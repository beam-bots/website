This one's a little late. I've just got back from [Goatmire](https://goatmire.com) in
Varberg, Sweden, via the most punishing jet lag I have ever experienced — the sort where
you wake up at 3am wide awake and fall asleep mid-sentence at 4pm. If anything below
trails off, that's why.

Short version: the workshop happened, the talk happened, and then the robots went home
with other people and immediately got better than the ones I'd built.

## Achieving Balance (Eventually)

The workshop was a little more chaotic than I'd planned for.

The plan was for it to be seamless: plug the Nerves Starter Kit into your laptop, run a
couple of mix tasks, and have it suddenly work. The reality was that two things stood
between a lot of laptops and that plan. `sunxi-tools` — which talks to the Allwinner chip
in FEL mode — didn't want to compile for a good fraction of the room. And the seamless
route into FEL mode went via the board's built-in CH340 USB-serial adapter, the same one
that gives you a serial console on the Linux system. Its macOS driver is a kernel
extension, which on current macOS means a parade of increasingly alarming security
dialogs. Not what you want to be talking people through with a room full of strangers'
laptops and four hours on the clock.

The punchline: the serial driver was only ever needed to flip the board into FEL mode, and
**there's a button on the board that does exactly that**. We always knew it was there — Gus
designed the board, after all — but I was so focused on the plug-it-in-and-it-works
experience that it didn't occur to either of us to reach for it as the escape hatch until
after the workshop. I'm choosing to regard this as a fun fact rather than a personal
failing.

Gus and I ended up flashing a pile of boards by hand so everyone could get to the fun
part, which they did. We didn't get all the way through the slides, so they're in the
repo for anyone who wants to finish the job at home:
[`bb_nsk/slides`](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_nsk/tree/main/slides), with a PDF if
you'd rather not build them.

The fixes we made in the trenches are already released in
[`bb_nsk`](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_nsk): 0.1.3 added Gus's `nsk` flashing tooling
to the installer and points it at our own forks of the flashing tools, and 0.1.4 adds
`rustler` so the one NIF without a prebuilt artefact can actually be built. Next time, the
button goes on the first slide anyway.

## Then The Robots Went Home And Got Better

This is my favourite part of the month, and I didn't do any of it.

Last week, [Gus Workman](https://github.com/gworkman) and
[Lars Wikman](https://github.com/lawik) (of the Goatmire clan) took their balance bots home
and had a proper play.

**Gus rewrote the control law**, and the robot now drives beautifully and shrugs off
disturbances — including, as it turns out, the scrutiny of a cat.

<div style="max-width: 360px; margin: 0 auto 1.5em;">
{{ <video src="/balance-bot-cat-gus.mp4" caption="A balance bot under feline inspection. Video by Gus Workman." /> }}
</div>

The clever bit is how it finds its balance point. A balance bot only stands still when it's
leaning at exactly the angle that puts its centre of mass over the wheels, and that angle
moves with the battery charge, the floor and whatever you've strapped to it. Get it
slightly wrong and the robot doesn't fall over — it just crawls steadily off in one
direction. Gus's velocity loop treats that crawl as the measurement: while nobody's driving
and the robot is moving slowly, a slow trim integrates the crawl back out and learns the
missing lean. A shove is fast enough to fall outside that window, so the robot doesn't
march back to where it was pushed from. And the trim is kept across falls, because the
balance point doesn't change just because the robot fell over.

**Then Lars gave it a face.** I'd hoped the display would be good for something like this,
and Lars went and proved it:

<div style="max-width: 360px; margin: 0 auto 1.5em;">
{{ <video src="/balance-bot-faces-lars.mp4" caption="Expressive. Video by Lars Wikman." /> }}
</div>

**Then Gus added a remote control**, using the AtomVM-based 2026 Goatmire badge. The badge
joins the robot as a node over Erlang distribution and sends `{:drive, self(), throttle,
turn}` messages to a registered process on the robot. There's no watchdog in the remote
code at all, because there doesn't need to be: the drive controllers already drop a request
after `drive_timeout`, so a badge that goes quiet stops the robot. That's the sort of thing
I like seeing — reaching for the framework's guarantee instead of writing another one.

<div style="max-width: 360px; margin: 0 auto 1.5em;">
{{ <video src="/balance-bot-badge-remote-gus.mp4" caption="Driving a balance bot from a Goatmire badge running AtomVM. Video by Gus Workman." /> }}
</div>

Two very different BEAMs, one `send/2`. Lovely.

There's more speed on the way, too: Gus and Frank Hunleth are working on getting the ARM32
JIT into `nerves_system_trellis`, the Nerves system the starter kit runs. The BEAM on that
board is currently interpreted, so the JIT should give us a major boost — welcome news on a
board where, as [last month's post](@/blog/progress-2026-09-01.md) showed, CPU headroom is
the difference between balancing and not.

Gus's improvements will be rolled back into the official `bb_nsk` project. In the
meantime, if you've got a balance bot and want to play, his firmware is at
[`gworkman/goatbot`](https://github.com/gworkman/goatbot).

## Two New Packages

Both of these were built for the workshop and are now on Hex:

- **[`bb_nsk`](https://hex.pm/packages/bb_nsk)** — board support for the Nerves Starter Kit
  and its balance bot add-on. An Igniter installer builds the robot up a subsystem at a
  time (`bb_nsk.add_wheels`, `add_imu`, `add_balance`, `add_display`, `add_web` and
  friends), and it ships the drivers, sensors and balance loop, the panel, the LEDs,
  networking and a web interface with a drive pad. There's a `doctor` task you run on the
  board when something isn't working, and a `cheat` task that runs every `add_*` step in
  one go for when the workshop clock is against you. Five releases in under a fortnight,
  which is roughly what happens when the deadline is a room full of people.

  It's about to get a major rework. The goal is that a single `mix igniter.install bb_nsk`
  takes you all the way to a working balance bot, with Gus's new control law built in.
  Watch this space.
- **[`bb_parameter_store_cubdb`](https://hex.pm/packages/bb_parameter_store_cubdb)** — a
  `BB.Parameter.Store` backed by [CubDB](https://hex.pm/packages/cubdb), so parameter
  changes survive a reboot. Rather than slotting a module into an existing DSL option, it
  ships a DSL extension of its own: opt in with
  `use BB, extensions: [BB.Parameter.Store.CubDB.Dsl]`.

## The Framework, Meanwhile

Conference prep doesn't leave much room for framework work, but a few things landed that
are worth knowing about.

### Cancel anything

**[`bb` 0.31.1](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb/releases/tag/v0.31.1)** lets anyone cancel a
running command, not just whoever started it
([#260](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb/pull/260)). Previously, if automation kicked off a
command, an operator in the dashboard had no way to stop it. LiveView, Kino and MCP had all
worked around this by keeping hold of the pids they'd started themselves. Commands now
register under their execution id, with `BB.Command.list/1`, `whereis/2` and `cancel/2` on
top, and a string encoding of the id so it survives JSON, URLs and DOM attributes.

Fixing that turned up that `executing_commands/1` had been reporting the *wrong* id all
along — the monitor reference rather than the execution id — so nothing could correlate a
listed command with its PubSub topic. [`bb_liveview` 0.3.4](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_liveview/releases/tag/v0.3.4),
[`bb_kino` 0.4.4](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_kino/releases/tag/v0.4.4) and
[`bb_mcp` 0.3.3](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_mcp/releases/tag/v0.3.3) all now cancel any
running command.

### One arm down shouldn't mean both arms down

**[`bb` 0.31.2](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb/releases/tag/v0.31.2)** came straight from
hardware ([#263](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb/pull/263)). With two SO-101s in one VM,
one arm's servo bus lost power. Within a few seconds the *other* arm had gone too, along
with the rest of the application.

The topology supervisor was `:permanent`, so once it exhausted its restart budget it was
started again, against hardware that still wasn't there, spending the *root's* budget until
that gave up too. And when the root went down, the safety controller deleted the robot from
its table — taking with it the `:error` state that had been latched precisely so someone
could see what went wrong. Not just unrecovered: forgotten.

It's `:transient` now. A robot whose hardware subtree gives up stays down, the robot stays
registered, the fault stays readable, nothing propagates to its neighbours, and
`Supervisor.restart_child/2` brings it back once a human has acknowledged it. **That is a
behaviour change**: a robot no longer quietly reattaches itself to hardware that vanished.
For anything with motors on it, I'd argue that was never recovery.

### A disarm that told fibs

The same power cut turned up something worse
([`bb_servo_feetech` #126](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_servo_feetech/pull/126)):
`BB.Safety.disarm/1` could return `:ok` while the servos were still holding torque. Safety
state said `:disarmed`; the arm was still stiff.

The controller registered its disarm handler with a *snapshot* of its servo list — empty at
startup — and re-registered on every servo it discovered. The safety controller keeps
handlers in an ETS bag, so they piled up rather than replacing each other: seven
registrations for a six-servo arm, each holding a different prefix of the list. A disarm
during discovery ran all of them, including the one holding `[]`, and writing torque-off to
an empty list of servos succeeds admirably.

The handler is now registered once and asks the controller for its *live* servo list as it
runs. An empty list or an unreachable controller is an error, and the robot stays in
`:error` rather than being told the hardware is safe. A related fix
([#118](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_servo_feetech/pull/118)) stops an actuator when its
controller dies, rather than carrying on accepting commands and dropping them into a table
that no longer exists. Both are in
[`bb_servo_feetech` 0.7.2](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_servo_feetech/releases/tag/v0.7.2)
and [`bb_servo_robotis` 0.6.3](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_servo_robotis/releases/tag/v0.6.3),
which had the same shape.

### Smaller things

- **[`bb_so101` 0.4.0](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_so101/releases/tag/v0.4.0)** (breaking)
  drops the gripper's 45° transmission offset
  ([#88](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_so101/pull/88)). Since 0.3.0 calibration anchors the
  gripper's zero to its closed stop, so the offset was being applied twice. **If your arm was
  calibrated on 0.3.0 or later, recalibrate the gripper and remove the offset together** —
  the README has the `--joint gripper` invocation. The upgrader deliberately doesn't do it
  for you, because the right answer depends on when the arm was last calibrated, which it
  can't see.
- **[`bb_mcp` 0.3.3](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_mcp/releases/tag/v0.3.3)** got an audit
  against the DSL ([#57](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_mcp/pull/57)), prompted by a report
  that joint limits weren't coming through. They weren't — and a robot that declared a
  command `category` got no MCP session at all, the state resource crashed whenever a
  command was running, and error replies couldn't be encoded. Four of six resources had no
  tests; they do now. [0.3.4](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_mcp/releases/tag/v0.3.4) hands
  back an execution id when a command outlives its tool call.
- **[`bb_pid_controller` 0.3.3](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_pid_controller/releases/tag/v0.3.3)**
  accepts `nx` 1.0 alongside 0.12, as does `bb_policy`.
- The shared CI workflow can now
  [install apt packages before compiling](https://github.com/beam-bots/.github/pull/49),
  because `bb_nsk` pulls in `nerves_runtime`, which wants `libmnl`, which the runner
  doesn't have. And every repo swapped `mix_audit` for `mix hex.audit`, which reports
  advisories straight from hex.pm rather than from a git checkout that quietly goes stale.

## Coming In `bb` 0.32

I'd hoped to get `bb` 0.32.0 out the door before this post, and didn't quite manage it.
Everything below is merged on `main` and will be in it. Heads up: **it includes breaking
changes to how actuators are commanded**, so if you drive actuators directly, read the
first two items.

- **Arm epochs** ([#271](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb/pull/271)). "Is the robot armed?"
  is not the same question as "has the robot been armed continuously since this command
  was created?" — and only the first was being asked. So a command from one arming session
  could still be applied in a later one, after a disarm that was supposed to make things
  safe. Each successful arm now allocates an epoch, the `BB.Actuator` send functions stamp
  it onto the outgoing message, and actuators refuse anything from an earlier session with
  `BB.Error.State.StaleEpoch`. If you build command messages by hand and publish them
  straight at an actuator, you'll need to stamp them with `BB.Safety.epoch/1` or switch to
  the send functions.
- **One delivery API for every actuator command**
  ([#272](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb/pull/272)). `set_position/4` took a `:delivery`
  option; the other five commands each had a plain function, a `!` that meant "cast" (the
  one variant that *can't* raise), and a `_sync`. They're all one function with
  `delivery: :pubsub | :broadcast | :direct` now, and the `!` and `_sync` variants are gone.
  Options are validated, so a typo'd option raises instead of doing nothing, and
  `reply_on_reject?: true` means a `:direct` send can finally hear about a refusal.
- **Estimator intake freshness** ([#270](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb/pull/270)) —
  `max_input_age`, per input or per estimator, and fixes for an estimator that could never
  recover from `:lost` without a `latency_budget`, an auxiliary input keeping a dead driver
  looking alive, and a latency measurement that mixed nanoseconds with native time units.
- **Validated command results** ([#277](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb/pull/277)). A
  command whose `result/1` returned a bare term took the runtime down, and the restart
  force-disarmed the robot. Now it just fails the command.
- **Two vocabulary pages** ([#280](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb/pull/280)), reworked from
  the workshop material: *Robotics Vocabulary* for Elixir developers who've never had to
  describe a physical machine, and *Elixir for Roboticists* for people coming the other way.
  `bb_pid_controller` gained a matching [explanation of PID from scratch](https://github.com/beam-bots/bb_pid_controller/pull/111),
  including how to tune one by watching what the robot does. If you've ever stared at a
  robot rocking back and forth and wondered which gain to blame, it's for you.
